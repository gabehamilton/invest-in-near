Sources in detail

The gotchas below are the ones that change a number if you miss them.

NEAR RPC

Archival JSON-RPC at archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com: block by height for total supply, validators for stake, EXPERIMENTAL_protocol_config for the issuance ceiling, and call_function on meta-pool.near and linear-protocol.near for liquid staking prices.

  • The config field protocol_reward_rate reads as a fraction, but nearcore hard-codes the treasury's share on mainnet. The figures use the declared constant treasury_share from thesis/constants.toml, pinned to the nearcore source line, so stakers get the remainder.
  • Stake from validators trails the current epoch, so the staked share lags slightly.
  • The archival endpoint rate-limits. The sample cache is append-only, a missing sample stays missing, and a window edge with no sample nearby is null.
  • The history file's last row is the latest block, not the start of a day. It sets the “now” figures (supply, staked share, the rate in force); trailing windows end at the latest start-of-day sample instead.

BigQuery public dataset

bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us, queried with the SQL in sql/, always filtered on block_date.

  • The dataset lives in us-east1, and token amounts are FLOAT64 in yoctoNEAR, so sums lose precision at the last digits.
  • Before the developer gas rebate ended (HSP-027, at block 223,012,337 on 2026-11-04), tokens_burnt overstates the burn, because part of every function-call fee went to the called contract as a reward. From that block the whole fee burns, so gross equals net. The exact burn comes from per-block changes in total supply.
  • The dataset stopped updating for long stretches. The status board shows its last block date, and the burn chart says which method it used.

FRED

One keyless fredgraph.csv?id= request per series: fed funds (DFF, EFFR), the target range, CPI, core CPI, PCE, money supply, the euro exchange rate and the Fed's median projections. The desk simulation adds two: the euro short-term rate, €STR (ECBESTRVOLWGTTRMDMNRT, the European Central Bank's volume-weighted overnight rate, which FRED republishes), and the one-year Treasury yield (DGS1).

  • The desk reads each rate on or before the latest date every input shares, the same rule as the effective fed funds rate, so no rate comes from a day the price data has not reached.
  • FRED serves the latest vintage only, so a revised month changes history on the next pull.
  • The BLS skipped one month of CPI during the federal shutdown. Lines break there, year-over-year values that need that month are null, and windows that start or end in it move to the nearest month inside the window.

BIS

Central bank policy rates (WS_CBPOL) and consumer price inflation (WS_LONG_CPI) for the currency panel.

  • Australia publishes CPI quarterly; the monthly series repeats each quarter's value, and the chart flags it.
  • Argentina is excluded: its central bank stopped publishing a policy rate.

Binance and Coinbase prices

Daily candles from data-api.binance.vision for NEAR, bitcoin and ether, and Coinbase's NEAR-USD as a cross-check and fallback.

  • Binance quotes against USDT, not dollars. The cross-check against Coinbase's dollar price measures the gap.
  • Coinbase's NEAR history starts later than Binance's, so a Coinbase fallback omits the early windows and lists them.

Derivatives venues

Deribit, OKX and Aevo instrument lists for options, and Deribit's volatility indexes for bitcoin and ether.

  • An unreachable venue reads “not checked”, never “no”, and counts separately.

DefiLlama

Daily NEAR Intents volume and fees from DefiLlama's summary/dexs/near-intents and summary/fees/near-intents.

  • DefiLlama's figures come from a Dune table maintained by NEAR, so they are not independent of Dune. The Explorer cross-check exists to test that.
  • The most recent days are provisional and often revised upward; the Intents volume chart uses complete months only.

Money for the 10 most-traded currencies

One public, keyless money series per currency, from the publisher that issues it: FRED for the dollar, the ECB for the euro, the Bank of Japan for the yen, the Bank of England for sterling, the OECD for the yuan, the SNB for the franc, the RBA for the Australian dollar, the Bank of Canada for the Canadian dollar, the HKMA for the Hong Kong dollar, and SingStat, via the MAS, for the Singapore dollar. Each raw file under data/raw/money/<currency>/ has a sidecar with its URL, units and fetch time.

  • Units differ by publisher (millions, billions, index points); the loader reads each sidecar's unit string and refuses a unit it does not know.
  • Each currency's money growth is its latest twelve months, ending at its latest published month; nothing is filled or carried forward.
  • The Bank of England refuses repeated requests, so the pull makes one paced request per series and keeps the previous file when refused.
  • The OECD's monetary aggregates mirror several national series; cross-check X9 compares them.

BIS Triennial Survey ranking

The 10 currencies are ranked by daily turnover from the survey annex's table of turnover by currency, net-net, read from the BIS statistics API (dataflow WS_DER_OTC_TOV) by pipeline/sources/bis_fx.py into data/raw/bis_fx/; the annex PDF is the cross-check. The code sorts and takes the top ten; nobody types the list. The ranking uses the annex's later vintage of the figures (the survey's press page carries an earlier total), from the survey month 2025-04, and holds until the next survey.

NEAR Intents Explorer

The partner API at explorer.near-intents.org/api/v0. It needs a partner access token from NEAR Intents and allows one request every few seconds.

  • Raw rows are aggregated in memory and never written to disk, per NEAR Intents Terms of Service, section 4.1(e).
  • Published aggregates wait for written permission; see licences and terms.

Formulas

One anchor per formula id. Every number in the registry names one of these. Ids: F is a computed value, D a value read directly from a source, K a declared constant, P a parameter set in thesis/rules.toml, and X, further down, a cross-check between two sources.

F-growth Supply growth over a window

g = (S1 / S0) ^ (365.25 / days) - 1

S is total supply from the RPC sample nearest each window edge, and days come from the two blocks' timestamps. Samples are the block at the start of a UTC day: monthly from launch, then weekly, then daily (rpc/history_samples.csv merged with rpc/dense_samples.csv; on a date both files sampled they must agree exactly). Every trailing window ends at the latest of these full-day samples, so with daily samples a trailing year spans exactly one year. If no sample sits within a week of an edge, the value is null, never approximated.

Used by: g_365, g_365_at_aud, g_365_at_cad, g_365_at_chf, g_365_at_cny, g_365_at_core, g_365_at_cpi, g_365_at_eur, g_365_at_gbp, g_365_at_hkd, g_365_at_jpy, g_365_at_m2, g_365_at_pce, g_365_at_sgd, g_365_at_usd, g_365_neg_run_days, g_y_lst_365

F-ceiling Most the protocol allowed over the same window

ceiling = exp( sum_k  i_k * dt_k ) - 1      (i_k = max_inflation_rate in force, dt_k in years)

The issuance ceiling compounded over the time each value was in force, from EXPERIMENTAL_protocol_config. The rate switches at the exact first block of each change, found by bisecting the config between two sampled blocks (rpc/protocol_changes.csv), so the ceiling is exact rather than bracketed.

Used by: ceiling_365, ceiling_gap_dir

F-burnshare Burn as a share of issuance, exact

burn_share = sum(burn) / sum(mint)

Mint and burn from per-block changes in BigQuery's total supply. Used only when coverage of the window is complete.

Used by: burn_method, burn_method_short, burn_per_day_365, burn_share_365, mint_per_day_365, n_burn_epochs_365

F-burn-epochs Burn as a share of issuance, exact from every epoch boundary

minted     = sum_b minted_b      over every epoch boundary b with start_height < b <= end_height
burnt      = minted - (supply(end) - supply(start))
burn_share = burnt / minted
minted_b   = supply(b) - supply(b - 1) + burn in block b

Total supply moves only by the mint at the first block of each epoch and by the burn in every block, so over the exact window between two midnight UTC samples a year apart the burn is the summed mint less supply growth. No epoch is sampled or skipped: rpc/epoch_mints.csv walks back from the tip one boundary at a time, and each row records the boundary before it (prev_boundary_height). The method is used only when those links run unbroken from a boundary at or before the window start to one after its end; a gap or a missing link falls back to the monthly sample, and the burn chart says why. It must sit at or below the upper bound (cross-check). Labeled “exact: every epoch boundary” wherever it appears.

Used by: burn_share_365_epochs

F-burn-sampled Burn as a share of issuance, one epoch per month

burn_share = sum_e burnt_e / sum_e minted_e      (one complete epoch e per calendar month)
burnt_e  = supply(start) - supply(end) + burn in the start block
minted_e = supply(next start) - supply(end) + burn in the next start block

Each sampled epoch's mint and burn are exact RPC reads (rpc/epoch_history.csv). Only one epoch a month is read, so a single unusual epoch stands in for its whole month: the burn chart lists every sampled epoch, keeps the outliers, and shows the upper bound beside the sampled share. Used when neither BigQuery nor every epoch boundary covers the period (the calendar years), kept beside an exact trailing-year share for comparison, and labeled “sampled: one epoch per month” wherever it appears.

Used by: burn_share_365_sampled, n_burn_epochs_365_sampled

F-mint-check Realized mint against the ceiling, per epoch

gap_e = minted_e * seconds_per_year / (supply(start) * epoch_seconds) - max_inflation_rate      (pp)

Since nearcore's RectifyInflation protocol version (declared constant rectify_inflation_protocol), an epoch's reward is the ceiling rate times supply times the epoch's duration, so a full-online epoch mints at the ceiling and validators below the online threshold leave part unminted. Earlier epochs were paid by block count and are listed but not compared. One complete epoch per month (rpc/epoch_history.csv).

Used by: mint_check_365_method, mint_check_from, mint_vs_ceiling_max_365_pp, mint_vs_ceiling_median_365_pp, mint_vs_ceiling_min_365_pp, n_mint_epochs_365

F-burn-ub Burn plus unminted, upper bound

burn + unminted <= S0 * (exp(i * years) - 1) - (S1 - S0)

The gap between what the ceiling would mint and what supply actually did bounds the burn from above: the true share cannot exceed it. It is the measured share only when neither BigQuery, every epoch boundary nor a month-by-month epoch sample covers the period, and it is drawn beside the measured share in every period. Labeled “upper bound” wherever it appears.

Used by: burn_share_365_ub

F-policy-latest What holding each currency pays now

pol_c = the latest end-of-month central bank policy rate for c's area      (BIS WS_CBPOL; the euro: the euro area)
pol_usd = the latest daily effective federal funds rate                  (FRED DFF)

The comparison economies' rates are in data/raw/bis/policy_rates_monthly.csv; China's and Hong Kong's come from the same BIS series in their own file, data/raw/bis/policy_rates_monthly_cn_hk.csv. For China, BIS's policy-rate series is the one-year Loan Prime Rate (its official one-year lending rate before the Loan Prime Rate replaced it); for Hong Kong it is the HKMA base rate, which follows the US rate under the peg. Desk simulation: method

The film's last scene, as a model. Its formulas are the F-sim anchors above; every figure here is the default state, and every one is in the registry below.

A trading desk holds $500 million it will not deploy for 365 days: 76% in dollar cash and 24% in euro cash. That split is a benchmark proxy from the two currencies' shares of foreign-exchange trading, not any desk's holdings. The mix moves 3.7% ($18.50 million) into liquid-staked NEAR, bought with dollars and euros in the same proportions, and the baseline keeps everything in cash. Both are bought and held, in nominal dollars; deflating both by the same price index would leave their difference the same in real terms.

Dollar cash earns the effective fed funds rate, 3.88% (4.01% over a year, compounded). Euro cash earns €STR, 2.44% (2.51%). The NEAR part earns NEAR's liquid-staking yield over the last year, 4.13%, paid in NEAR. Every rate is held where it is today. The one-year Treasury, 4.38% (4.43% as an annual rate), replaces fed funds only in one bracket cell and one sensitivity row.

Each of the 20,000 simulated years is 365 days drawn at random, with replacement, from the last 365 days to 2026-11-09, each draw keeping NEAR's move and the euro's move from the same day; the alternative window is the last 90 days. Each day's move is divided by the window's average move, so the window's trend is gone and its swings remain, and each year is then scaled so the average year ends where the price assumption says. As a check, the simulated years spread NEAR's price by 103.5% (the standard deviation of its log), against a measured volatility of 103.3% over the last year.

The expected benefit is exact, from the closed form; the typical year, the tails and the dots come from the simulated years. The price change is the reader's assumption, and the default is flat on average. One year of data pins an average price change only to within about 103.3% either way, so no past return is offered as a forecast. The other assumptions on offer: demand flat in dollars (−2.42%) and the thesis's implication (3.15% from money, 1.20% from prices), which assumes demand for NEAR keeps pace with dollar money, something nothing on this site measures.

What this leaves out

  • An exchange or custodian failing.
  • The liquid-staking contract failing, or its token trading below what it redeems for.
  • Rates changing during the year: every rate is held where it is today.
  • Trading and custody costs, unless entered above.
  • Accounting and capital treatment: ask your auditor and your regulator.
  • A crypto desk needs cash most when crypto is falling, which is when NEAR is likely down and thin.

The desk's charts

  • c30 100 simulated years for the $18.50 million NEAR part: ahead in 30, behind in 70
  • c31 More NEAR scales the outcome, not the odds: ahead in about 30 of 100 simulated years at every share
  • c32 Getting out: about 3.1 days to sell the $18.50 million NEAR part at a whole median day of Coinbase volume
  • c33 What moves the answer: rates and staking move the expected value, the window moves the spread

Known gaps and unverified items

  • Slashing unverified · affects H6, c14

    As far as I can tell NEAR does not slash validator stake today, but I have not confirmed it in the protocol spec or the nearcore source. If slashing exists, liquid staking carries a loss path that none of the yield figures include.

    Would resolve it: Cite the nearcore code path or the Nomicon section that governs slashing, pinned to a commit.

    Nomicon, the NEAR protocol specification

  • LiNEAR instant-exit depth not measured · affects H6, c14

    Meta Pool's instant-unstake pool holds 75,887 NEAR, read from its contract. LiNEAR's equivalent was not measured, so the same-day liquidity row of the sweep checklist is marked partial.

    Would resolve it: Read LiNEAR's instant-unstake liquidity and fee from its contract at the same height as the Meta Pool read.

  • Binance and Bybit options, not checkable from the US not checkable · affects H8, c15

    Binance's options API and Bybit's public endpoints refuse requests from US addresses, so the venue table marks them not checked rather than no. Unchecked venues: 2.

    Would resolve it: A venue check from a non-US network, with each venue's HTTP status.

  • Purpose of the treasury outflow unverified

    The treasury.near balance, column treasury_near in data/raw/rpc/history_samples.csv, dropped sharply in one quarter this year. I could not find a published purpose. Supply growth is unaffected; sell pressure might not be.

    Would resolve it: A treasury report or an on-chain trace that names where the NEAR went.

  • Origin of the quoted staking rate unverified · affects H3, c01

    The hypothesis quotes a staking rate without saying where it came from. The ledger tests the claim against the measured trailing-year liquid staking yield, 4.13%, and shows both.

    Would resolve it: Name the dashboard and the date the quoted rate came from.

  • BigQuery freshness depends on BigQuery · affects H10, c05, c16

    Exact burn figures need the public NEAR BigQuery dataset, whose latest block date is 2026-11-10; where it has no data the figure is labelled sampled or upper bound.

    Would resolve it: The BigQuery dataset keeping pace with the chain (the data page shows its lag in days).

    NEAR docs: BigQuery public dataset

  • Permission to publish Intents Explorer aggregates open · affects c06

    The NEAR Intents Explorer comparison needs partner access and written permission to publish under its terms. It is not included, so the Intents volume chart rests on DefiLlama alone.

    Would resolve it: Partner access and a written answer from NEAR Intents.

    NEAR Intents Explorer API documentation

  • Bank of England money series open · affects c21

    Sterling money here is the wide aggregate the Bank publishes as M4; the narrower version that excludes intermediate financial companies is the closer match for other currencies' broad money, but its series code is not confirmed.

    Would resolve it: The confirmed Bank of England series code for the narrower aggregate.

Terms notes by source

NEAR mainnet JSON-RPC
Public JSON-RPC; chain data is public. FastNEAR rate-limits archival calls (HTTP 429), so samples are cached append-only.
FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
Keyless FRED graph CSV. FRED terms of use apply; each series is credited to its original publisher (BLS, BEA, Federal Reserve Board, New York Fed). FRED keeps no vintages here, and BLS published no October 2025 CPI.
BIS data portal (policy rates, including China and Hong Kong; consumer prices)
Keyless BIS SDMX API; BIS statistics may be reused with the source credited. Argentina has no policy rate after 2025-07-10; Australia's CPI is quarterly.
Binance spot klines (NEARUSDT, BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT)
Binance public market-data host; Binance terms of use apply. Quotes are in USDT, not USD (cross-checked against Coinbase NEAR-USD).
Coinbase Exchange candles (NEAR-USD)
Coinbase Exchange public market data; Coinbase terms apply. Series starts 2022-09-01.
Options venue check (Deribit, OKX, Aevo, Bybit, Binance)
Public exchange APIs. Bybit and Binance block US addresses; an unreachable venue is recorded as not checked, never as no.
Deribit DVOL (BTC, ETH implied volatility)
Deribit public API; context only (no NEAR implied volatility exists).
DefiLlama (NEAR Intents volume and fees)
DefiLlama open API. The volume adapter reads a Dune table maintained by NEAR, so it is not independent of Dune; the 3 most recent days are provisional.
NEAR Intents Explorer API (1Click daily aggregates)
1ClickSwap API Terms 4.1(e): API data may not be stored for more than 24 hours, so rows never leave memory and only daily aggregates are written. Publishing them waits for written permission from NEAR Intents.
Fiat money panel: central banks and their statistics portals (FRED, ECB, BoJ, BoE, OECD, SNB, RBA, BoC, HKMA, SingStat)
Keyless public statistics; each series is credited to its publisher on the data page. The BoE and HKMA responses are archived unchanged under each currency's _raw folder; every other series records the full request URL and the SHA-256 of the response.
BIS Triennial Central Bank Survey 2025 (FX turnover by currency)
Keyless BIS SDMX API; BIS statistics may be reused with the source credited. Picks the ten most-traded currencies for the money panel.
BigQuery public dataset crypto_near_mainnet_us
Google Cloud public dataset published by NEAR; query outputs only are committed. tokens_burnt overstates the supply burn by the developer gas rebate (burnt_gas_reward) only before HSP-027 took effect (the burnt_gas_reward change in rpc/protocol_changes.csv); from that block gross equals net.
Declared constants (nearcore, core-contracts, eCFR, press)
Quoted under fair use with links to pinned source revisions.
