# NEAR, tested

**Relative to the dollar and the major currencies, NEAR's supply grows more slowly, and its supply-and-staking edge over the dollar has held as rates moved. It is not cash.**

**The claim under test:** “NEAR is deflationary relative to the dollar and other currencies.”

I split the hypothesis behind that sentence into claims and tested each one, and the assumption hiding inside them, against public data on today's economy. Of the 9 stated claims I could test: supported 5, partly 3, refuted 1. The hidden assumption, that a yield paid in NEAR compares with a yield paid in dollars, fails. Two pieces carry the thesis. NEAR's supply grows slower than the major currencies' money, by a median +2.31 pp. Staking ran 0.32 pp above compounded fed funds over the past year. Thin, but there. The claim that NEAR suits sweep balances fails its own test in the ledger below.

_Data as of 2026-11-14 03:26 UTC. Every figure comes from `site/data/numbers.json`, where each one has its formula, inputs and raw files. The charts are on the site, https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/ (`site/index.html` in this repository), with sources and formulas on its data page._

## The claim

NEAR is deflationary relative to the dollar and other currencies. Its supply grows slower than the money of the 10 most-traded currencies, by a median +2.31 pp over each one's latest year. Staking ran 0.32 pp above compounded fed funds over the past year.

**Scope.** I judge NEAR the way someone deciding today whether to hold it would, on the economy as it is now. Governance is left out, past and future: if the rules change, holders hear about it well in advance and can sell. Past prices count where they say something about the coming year, so most tests use the latest year of data.

**Strongest:** Supply, against money. In the year to 2026-11-10 NEAR's supply grew 2.48%, while the major currencies' money grew a median 4.79%, each over its own latest year: a median gap of +2.31 pp. Chart [c21](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#c21).

**Weakest:** Staking ran only 0.32 pp above compounded fed funds over the year, the thinnest margin, in percentage points, of any supported claim. The ninety-day tripwire on this spread fires if staking falls below fed funds. Chart [c01](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#c01).

There is no price target and no valuation model here. The thesis covers supply and yield, and stops before demand and price.

## Terms

Key: H is a claim as I wrote it, O an objection, A a question from the hypothesis with the claims that answer it, T a tripwire and c a chart.

- **pp (percentage points)**: The plain difference between two rates. Staking at a yield a little above fed funds is a spread of a fraction of a pp.
- **Epoch**: One validator round on NEAR, about 6.99 h long at the moment. The protocol mints new NEAR once per epoch.
- **Issuance ceiling**: The protocol's max_inflation_rate: the most new NEAR it mints in a year, as a share of supply. It is 2.50% today, and governance sets it.
- **Liquid staking token (stNEAR, LiNEAR)**: A receipt for staked NEAR whose price in NEAR rises as staking rewards accrue. Meta Pool issues stNEAR and LiNEAR Protocol issues LiNEAR. Liquid staking yield is the growth of that price.
- **Fed funds compounded**: What a dollar earning the effective fed funds rate every day of a window would have made. It is the like-for-like benchmark for a staking yield over the same days.
- **Spread**: Staking yield minus fed funds over the same days, in pp.
- **Fee burn**: NEAR destroyed when transactions pay for gas. Burn offsets issuance, and supply shrinks only when burn exceeds what the protocol mints.
- **Upper bound (burn)**: The most the burn can be, from supply alone: what the ceiling would have minted minus what supply grew by. It counts rewards left unminted by offline validators as burn, so the true burn cannot exceed it.
- **CPI (CPI-U) and core CPI**: The US consumer price index for all urban consumers, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Core leaves out food and energy.
- **US money supply**: The Federal Reserve's broad money measure: currency, checking and savings deposits, small time deposits and retail money funds.
- **Realized volatility**: The annualized standard deviation of daily price changes. A bigger number means bigger typical swings.
- **Sweep account rules**: The SEC's money market fund rule and its broker customer-protection rule, which set what a broker may sweep idle cash into.
- **Implied volatility**: The volatility an options price assumes. Implied minus realized is, on average, what an option seller earns for taking the risk.
- **Call option**: The right to buy at a set price by a set date. Its price rises with the swings the market expects before that date.
- **Rebalancing**: Trading back to a fixed mix at set intervals, here the desk's share in NEAR and the rest in dollars: selling some NEAR after it rises and buying some after it falls. The desk simulation runs the same strategy against dollars and euros and calls it resetting the NEAR part to its share.
- **Shuffled history**: NEAR's own daily price moves, cut into blocks of 30 days and put back in a random order. It keeps every move, the volatility and the overall trend and changes only their order, so a strategy's result on it is what chance gives.
- **Perpetual future**: A contract that tracks NEAR's price and never expires. Buyers and sellers post collateral, and one side pays the other funding to keep the contract's price near the spot price.
- **Perpetual-style future**: A US-regulated form of a perpetual, listed on a futures exchange: Coinbase Derivatives' NEAR contract sets a funding rate every hour like a perpetual but carries an expiry date years away, and it is margined in dollars.
- **Short**: A bet that the price falls: sell now, buy back later. A short on a perpetual is settled in dollars, so the NEAR it hedges stays staked.
- **Perp ladder**: The third way of selling NEAR's swings: keep the NEAR staked and sell 10% of the stake short on a perpetual after each 10% rise, up to 50%, buying each slice back after a 10% fall. It gains when NEAR swings and comes back, and loses when NEAR runs one way.
- **Funding**: The payment between the two sides of a perpetual, settled every hour or every eight hours by each venue's formula. Hyperliquid's and Binance's formulas add a fixed interest term, so shorts are paid a set rate whenever the perpetual trades close to the spot price.
- **Short that never trades**: What the perp ladder is measured against: a short of 25% of the stake, sold at the start of a year and bought back at its end, with the same dollars on the venue. What the ladder gains over it is what trading the swings added.
- **Redrawn history**: Blocks of 30 days of NEAR's own days drawn at random with repeats, so some months appear twice and some not at all, then shifted so their average matches the real result: they show how far a result moves with the months drawn.
- **Tripwire**: A measured result that would change the thesis, checked against the latest data. Some would break part of it; others would strengthen it.

## The thesis that survives

This is for someone who holds NEAR or is deciding whether to. NEAR's supply grows every epoch, 2.48% over the year to 2026-11-10, but more slowly than money: the dollar's money grew 5.71% over the year to 2026-09, and 9 of the 10 most-traded currencies grew their money faster than NEAR's supply, each over its own latest year. Since the issuance ceiling cut, NEAR's supply grew 2.48% a year against 4.17% for US consumer prices. Staking ran 0.32 pp above compounded fed funds, and against other currencies' policy rates the result is mixed. Add the two gaps, which leave NEAR's dollar price out, and NEAR's edge over the dollar was at or below zero in 0 of 42 months across 4 separate years ending before and after the cut. In dollars, NEAR is volatile. No options venue I could check lists NEAR, so there is no option premium to collect; selling its swings on a perpetual made +11.02 pp a year on Hyperliquid and -6.91 pp on Binance against a short that never trades, and which months are drawn sets the sign. None of the three ways of selling it beat holding by more than chance gives.

**What holding pays, and where staked NEAR ranks**. 7 of the 9 most-traded currencies with a policy rate pay less now than liquid staking NEAR paid over the last year, 4.13%. That sets each central bank's latest reported rate beside a past year's staking yield, and a policy rate is a benchmark that cash rarely earns in full; the like-for-like comparison is the one above. China's rate is the one-year Loan Prime Rate and Hong Kong's the HKMA base rate, which follows the US rate under the peg. A currency with no policy rate is left out. Ranked by the yearly rate holding pays, staked NEAR comes 3 of 10: its liquid-staking yield over the year to 2026-11-10, 4.13%, against each currency's latest policy rate, the dollar's being fed funds now. A tie would rank the currency ahead. Staking pays in NEAR, so its dollar value moves with NEAR's price.

### The objections, answered

**[O1](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#O1) “Supply goes up every epoch.”** Stance: Answered on discipline. True, and the thesis is relative: it compares NEAR's supply growth with money and prices. The growth follows the protocol's ceiling: 2.48% over the last year, against a ceiling of 2.50%.

**[O2](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#O2) “Staking yield is just dilution. You're paying yourself.”** Stance: Answered for stakers. Only if everyone stakes. Staking pays more than new supply adds, so a liquid staker's share of all NEAR grew 1.61% a year. The cost lands on unstaked holders, whose share changed by -2.42% a year.

**[O3](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#O3) “Then there's no real yield. Fees don't cover issuance.”** Stance: Conceded. Fees burned 1.51% of the NEAR minted over the last year (exact), 1,337 NEAR a day against 88,303 NEAR minted. The staking yield is mostly a transfer from unstaked holders.

**[O4](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#O4) “So where does long-term value come from? Show me demand.”** Stance: Open. The thesis makes no claim about demand. Intents volume is shown as context for the fee burn: NEAR Intents settled $32.9 billion over the past year, and the latest ninety days ran at 1.84× the ninety days before (the latest 3 days are provisional and may be revised). Value that moves as stablecoins and other tokens holds no NEAR, so it reaches the token through fees, and the burn above is thin.

**[O5](https://gabehamilton.github.io/invest-in-near/index.html#O5) “Your yield is in NEAR. A dollar investor eats all of that volatility.”** Stance: Conceded for dollar holders. Measured in dollars, NEAR's price swung 103.3% annualized over the last year, and at its worst it sat 65.5% below its high within that year. Staking ran 0.32 pp above fed funds for the whole year, while a typical single day moved the price 5.41%.

## Desk simulation

A scenario, not a forecast: part of a trading desk's idle dollars and euros moved into liquid-staked NEAR for one year. The default outputs below assume NEAR's price is flat on average, daily moves like the last year's, the NEAR part reset to its share every 30 days, and no trading or custody costs; the page lets a reader change each assumption.

Each default output, beside the assumption it rests on:

- The desk: $500 million held for 365 days, 76% in dollar cash at the effective fed funds rate (3.88%) and 24% in euro cash at €STR (2.44%). Assumes rates stay where they are today; the split is a benchmark proxy from the two currencies' shares of currency trading, not any desk's holdings.
- The NEAR part: 3.7% of the desk, $18.50 million, liquid-staked at 4.13% and bought with dollars and euros in the cash's own proportions. Assumes the largest share whose bad-year average, held without trading, fits inside one year of the whole desk's interest, and resets the NEAR part to that share every 30 days.
- Expected benefit: $90,000 ahead of dollars and euros only; the yield over cash, before any price change, is $90,000 of it, and $70,000 of that is the euro-dollar rate gap (euro cash earns less than dollar cash), so against dollar cash alone staking adds $20,000. Assumes NEAR's price is flat on average and no trading or custody costs; it is exact, not simulated.
- The same expected benefit under the other price assumptions: $380,000 behind if demand for NEAR is flat in dollars, $700,000 ahead if the thesis's implication holds (3.15%, which assumes demand for NEAR keeps pace with dollar money).
- Typical year: $1 million behind. Assumes the next year's daily moves look like the last year's, a volatility of 103.3%, over 20,000 simulated years.
- Ahead of dollars and euros only in 47 of 100 simulated years; the NEAR share moves it only a little.
- Bad year: 5 in 100 simulated years end worse than $31 million behind, and those years average $38 million behind, about 2.1 years of the whole desk's interest.
- For the mix to come out ahead in most years, NEAR's price would need to rise about 5% on average. A round-trip trading cost above 0.20% of each amount the resets trade, with custody as entered, would erase the expected benefit; the resets trade $51.2 million over the year.
- Getting out: about 3.1 days to sell the NEAR part at a whole median day of Coinbase volume, before unstaking.
- Held without trading instead: typical year $8.0 million behind, ahead in 30 of 100, bad-year average $17.80 million. In these simulated years the resets change the expected benefit by -$180; H10 tests them on NEAR's real history.
- Why the desk resets: three ways of selling NEAR's swings were run on the one-year windows they all share: selling after a 25% rise and waiting in dollars (selling into rallies), resetting the NEAR part to its share every 30 days, and the perp ladder (shorting slices of the stake on a perpetual future as NEAR rises and buying them back as it falls). Resetting came out ahead of the other two on both venues' spans. In percentage points (pp) of the staked NEAR's value against simply holding it, over Binance's 1,317 shared windows it ended +0.21 pp, against -16.23 pp for the perp ladder and -42.20 pp for selling into rallies; over Hyperliquid's 741, +21.71 pp against +16.88 pp and +10.94 pp. None of the three beat chance against NEAR's shuffled histories, so the order says which suited NEAR's past, not which captures its swings.

This is a scenario tool, not investment advice. It shows what the measured yield, volatility and liquidity imply under an assumption you choose; it does not predict NEAR's price.

## Limits and open questions

What this page cannot settle yet, and what would settle each item.

- **Slashing** (unverified). As far as I can tell NEAR does not slash validator stake today, but I have not confirmed it in the protocol spec or the nearcore source. If slashing exists, liquid staking carries a loss path that none of the yield figures include. Would resolve it: Cite the nearcore code path or the Nomicon section that governs slashing, pinned to a commit.
- **LiNEAR instant-exit depth** (not measured). Meta Pool's instant-unstake pool holds 75,887 NEAR, read from its contract. LiNEAR's equivalent was not measured, so the same-day liquidity row of the sweep checklist is marked partial. Would resolve it: Read LiNEAR's instant-unstake liquidity and fee from its contract at the same height as the Meta Pool read.
- **Binance and Bybit options, not checkable from the US** (not checkable). Binance's options API and Bybit's public endpoints refuse requests from US addresses, so the venue table marks them not checked rather than no. Unchecked venues: 2. Would resolve it: A venue check from a non-US network, with each venue's HTTP status.
- **A NEAR perpetual for US holders, too new to test** (open). The ladder is tested on Hyperliquid and Binance, which are reported to exclude US persons (neither's terms were read first-hand here), and Kraken's own instrument record lists only non-US platforms for its NEAR perpetual. Coinbase Derivatives has listed a US-regulated NEAR perpetual-style future since 2026-02-28, which US customers trade in dollars through Coinbase Financial Markets, so an approved US holder can run the ladder there. Coinbase's help pages, read 2026-11-13, say access needs an approved application and an eligible address, set position limits by income and net worth, and may not be available in every jurisdiction; they name no US state as excluded. Its record holds 259 days of daily bars, short of the one-year window the test needs, and Coinbase publishes its funding history only on request, so neither claim reads it. Would resolve it: A year of Coinbase's NEAR perpetual record with its hourly funding, run through the same test as Hyperliquid and Binance.
- **near.com's perpetual market and fees** (unverified). near.com's own perpetual market list and fees could not be read; the test uses Hyperliquid's public book, which near.com says it routes to. Would resolve it: near.com's NEAR perpetual market and fee schedule, read first-hand.
- **Kraken's funding rate period** (unverified). Kraken's published funding rate is used only for its sign, because the period it is quoted for was not confirmed first-hand. Would resolve it: Kraken's documentation or a first-hand read that states the period its NEAR funding rate is quoted for.
- **Purpose of the treasury outflow** (unverified). The treasury.near balance, column treasury_near in data/raw/rpc/history_samples.csv, dropped sharply in one quarter this year. I could not find a published purpose. Supply growth is unaffected; sell pressure might not be. Would resolve it: A treasury report or an on-chain trace that names where the NEAR went.
- **Origin of the quoted staking rate** (unverified). The hypothesis quotes a staking rate without saying where it came from. The ledger tests the claim against the measured trailing-year liquid staking yield, 4.13%, and shows both. Would resolve it: Name the dashboard and the date the quoted rate came from.
- **BigQuery freshness** (depends on BigQuery). Exact burn figures need the public NEAR BigQuery dataset, whose latest block date is 2026-11-10; where it has no data the figure is labelled sampled or upper bound. Would resolve it: The BigQuery dataset keeping pace with the chain (the data page shows its lag in days).
- **Permission to publish Intents Explorer aggregates** (open). The NEAR Intents Explorer comparison needs partner access and written permission to publish under its terms. It is not included, so the Intents volume chart rests on DefiLlama alone. Would resolve it: Partner access and a written answer from NEAR Intents.
- **Bank of England money series** (open). Sterling money here is the wide aggregate the Bank publishes as M4; the narrower version that excludes intermediate financial companies is the closer match for other currencies' broad money, but its series code is not confirmed. Would resolve it: The confirmed Bank of England series code for the narrower aggregate.
